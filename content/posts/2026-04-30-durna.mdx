> El hünäri il gezermiş, aň hünäri Dünýäni!<br />
> Sen Watansyz hasap etme gonup-göçen durnany,<br />
> Onuň üçin serhet bolmaz, ol dünýäň bendiwany...<br />
> Bir-birine gollar beren illere gözüm düşdi.

Gurbannazar Ezizow, from *Gözüm düşdi* — every stanza closes on that same
phrase, *my eyes fell upon*.

He opens by weighing two kinds of work against each other. The proverb says a
craftsman's skill travels the land; he answers that the mind's craft travels the
world. Same sentence, two different sizes, and he is not shy about which one he
rates higher.

Then the crane, which isn't really about birds. Don't call it homeless for
landing and moving on — no border applies to it, it belongs to the world entire.
That is the shape of a thought as much as a migration. Nothing you think has
papers to show.

And the last line is where he lands: of everything his eye falls on, the thing
worth naming is peoples holding out a hand to one another. The mind's work
reaches everywhere; what makes it worth having is where it's joined to someone
else's. The full poem is on
[serpay](https://serpay.penjire.com/p/gurbannazar-ezizow/gozum-dusdi).
